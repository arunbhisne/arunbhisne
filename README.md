## I start with the work, not the brief.

Every project begins with me sitting next to the people doing the job and watching it. Where they wait, and where their judgement matters, is where AI fits. I run Nano Sapiens, an AI product studio, and these are three systems I built there.

**[Mapping Studio](https://www.arunbhisne.com/projects/mapping-studio)** · logistics
A senior analyst spent 20 to 24 hours matching a new customer's data fields to his company's own. Now the AI drafts it in about 8 minutes, and the analyst reviews and signs off. It went live 9 days after the build started.

**[EDI Copilot](https://www.arunbhisne.com/projects/edi-copilot)** · supply-chain compliance
Analysts checked invoice files against retailer rulebooks of up to 200 pages, and each check took 30 to 50 minutes. In the live pilot it takes 8. Confidential values are masked before the AI sees anything: 2,375 masked, none sent.

**[Chalkline](https://www.arunbhisne.com/projects/chalkline)** · a primary school, pro bono
It turns a textbook lesson into a narrated video for children aged 4 to 10, with 7 points where a person signs off. I let the AI decide only where reasoning actually helped, and that cut the projected first-phase cost from about 18 to 20 lakh rupees to about 1 lakh.

## How I build

I design the system and direct the build with AI coding agents (Claude Code, Cursor). Every project has a written brief, a definition of done and a decision log, and I audit what the agents write as if a stranger wrote it.

These are client projects, so the code stays private. The case studies show the problem, the decisions and the results. If you want to see how one was built, I'm happy to walk you through it on a call.

## I give names to the problems I keep running into.

- **The verification gap.** AI writes faster than anyone can read, so careful review quietly turns into skimming.
- **Trust drift.** The longer a tool is usually right, the less closely you check it.
- **The agency budget.** Every step that lets the model decide costs money and risk. Spend it only where reasoning adds something.
